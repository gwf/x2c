This is the second idea. The pieces compound. A class gets its methods and
its printing for free, a protocol gives it operators, a package gives it a
namespace, and each one builds on the one before, so a server, a job
pipeline, or a threaded tool is a few lines. The notes service on this
page is 209 lines, and it was measured against Go.

That is the superpower. What you would write in Go, Python, or JavaScript,
you write in x2c in about the same number of lines, and it runs at C
speed. In the
[language shootout](https://github.com/gwf/x2c/blob/main/examples/shootout/README.md),
fourteen problems written in x2c ran at a median of 1.04 times C's time,
and Python took 39 times. Links against everything you already have.
